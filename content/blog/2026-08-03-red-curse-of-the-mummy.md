The next story in [The Supernatural Writings of Stuart Palmer](/pages/stuart-palmer/) is up!

In [**The Red Curse of the Mummy**](/stuart-palmer/red-curse-of-the-mummy/), Ted Orchards recounts his experiences during an expedition to excavate the tomb of the Pharoah Amenophis ([Akhenaten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhenaten)). It did not go well.

The story's author is given as "Theodore Orchards," making this an ostensibly first-hand account. James Sieger's [1973 *Ghost Stories* index](https://archive.org/details/Ghost_Stories_Stories_of_Ghosts_1973_OPAR_pamphlet_on_Ghost_Stories_pulp/page/n3/mode/2up) identifies Orchards as a probable pseudonym for Stuart Palmer. Sieger notes that "[Editor Harold] Hersey spilled [the] beans in Apr. 1931 editorial." 

Despite the title, there really isn't much mummy in this story. It's better described as a "pharoah's curse" tale. The idea of such a curse gained a lot of traction a few years before this story appeared, after the opening of Tutankhamun's tomb in late 1922. The leader of that expedition, Lord Carnavon, died about four months after the tomb's opening; his death, and several others, were rumored to have been caused by a curse against the descecrators of the tomb. 

The death in this tale is a bit more dramatic---and much less ambiguous---than the deaths of King Tut's supposed victims. It's not one of Palmer's best ghost stories, in my opinion, and the native workers are portrayed in a rather condescending way. Still, a cursed tomb is a good pulpy subject; and at least I learned who Akhenanten was. If you like classic pulp, give this one a try.
