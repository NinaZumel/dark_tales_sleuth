Last post, I mentioned two Stuart Palmer pieces from the April 1930 issue of *Ghost Stories*: one I had, and one I didn't. Well now I have the second piece: "Between Two Worlds," the original version of "How Lost Was My Father?" from *Fate* July 1953.

[**You can read "Between Two Worlds" here.**](/stuart-palmer/between-two-worlds/)

This story of a man who mysteriously disappeared while walking in a field is based upon *three* Ambrose Bierce tales: "The Difficulty of Crossing a Field," "An Unfinished Race," and "Charles Ashmore's Trail"—mostly upon the first and third. All three of these tales can be found in the Bierce collection [*Can Such Things Be?*](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/ambrose-bierce/can-such-things-be).


I've now read both the *Ghost Stories* and *Fate* versions of the story; Palmer did revise it a little for its second publication, changing some details and expanding upon others. The housekeeper character Mammy Sue becomes Sukie; a pivotal seance moves from Brooklyn to Philadelphia. But the storylines are identical, and the *Fate* version reuses some of the key illustrations from the original. It retains the first-person narration of the *Ghost Stories* version, as well.

Reading it again, it struck me how eerie this story is. The narrator was a little girl when her father vanished literally into thin air; losing a parent so irrevocably would be one of the scariest experiences possible for a child. And the idea that her father might have spent half a century stuck in some kind of alternate-dimension limbo is just....*brrrrrr*.

It was also nice to read this story after having read several of Palmer's drier "non-fiction" articles (by which I mean his retellings of previously published paranormal accounts). I'd forgotten how atmospherically written Palmer's supernatural tales are. This one has a much quieter ending than stories like "Seven Gray Wolves" or "The Monster in the Cellar," but it was still lovely to read. I hope you enjoy it, too.
